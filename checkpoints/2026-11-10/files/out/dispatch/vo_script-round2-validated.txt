When an AI listens to a Texas doctor's visit and drafts the note, who checks it before it goes in the record?
A September post from the medical school at UTHealth Houston says UT Physicians is working toward adopting Epic's ambient listening platform as a unified system, after pilots of DAX and Abridge.
Its plans call for physicians across the practice to eventually get it, along with an Epic refresher.
On October 8th, 2026, a company called Abridge announced an agreement with the University of Texas System's health AI initiative.
Abridge says the agreement is a framework that lets UT health institutions access Abridge, and each one keeps its own decision.
Abridge says its tool maps the AI's draft back to source data, so a clinician can verify it.
Texas law, in effect since September 1st, 2025, lets a practitioner use AI for diagnosis under conditions that include reviewing all records created with AI.
That law is written around diagnosis, and the bill text doesn't say whether a visit note counts.
No source here says how often drafts are checked, or how accurate they are.
The vendor's design and the statute's review condition both point the check at the clinician. None of these sources says how well that check works.
