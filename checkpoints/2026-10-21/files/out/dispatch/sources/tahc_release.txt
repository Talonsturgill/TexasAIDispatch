TAHC Releases First Eligible New World Screwworm Infested Zones
Movement Restrictions Removed from Infested Zones 02.A and 03

AUSTIN, TX – The Texas Animal Health Commission (TAHC) executive director oﬃcially released movement restrictions
for areas of Texas previously deemed as New World screwworm (NWS) Infested Zones. Following months of
coordinated surveillance and eradication activities, designated areas of Gillespie, Kerr, Kimble, La Salle, and Webb
Counties were released from New World Screwworm (NWS) movement restrictions on August 31, 2026, marking a
signiﬁcant milestone in Texas' ongoing NWS response.
The executive director orders recognize robust response activities in these areas, lending to science-backed conﬁdence
in the release of these restrictions. View the oﬃcial release orders for zone 02.A and 03. The comprehensive eradication
strategy implemented by state, federal, industry, veterinary, and local partners delivered suﬃcient evidence that the
wild NWS population in these aﬀected areas have been eliminated.
“We are pleased with the incredible work and cooperation that has led us to the release of these ﬁrst zones,” said Dr.
Bud Dinges, TAHC executive director and Texas state veterinarian. “It has taken everyone’s diligence and focused eﬀorts
to get us to this point in the response. While we are certainly celebrating this milestone, we can’t take our eye oﬀ the
ball and must remain vigilant in monitoring animals and reporting any suspicions in these newly released areas and
beyond.”
The updated NWS interactive map is available on the TAHC NWS landing page and continues to reﬂect current zones
with NWS movement requirements in place. Animal owners and producers should continue to stay up to date on the
current situation, remain aware of how to prevent NWS infestations, and report any suspicious wounds or infestations
immediately.
TAHC, USDA, and other response partners may continue to conduct regulatory activities in areas released from NWS
Infested Zones. These activities can include sterile ﬂy releases, ﬂy surveillance trapping, and animal surveillance.
Landowners are encouraged to continue to monitor domestic animals and wildlife as often as possible for signs of odd
behavior, wounds, and larval infestations.
All suspicions of NWS infestations must be reported to a TAHC Region Office or 1-800-55-8242, Texas Parks and Wildlife
Department wildlife biologist or 512-389-4505, or your private veterinarian. Learn more about NWS, the statewide
situation, and more through the TAHC or USDA website.
The executive director order for releasing these areas is issued pursuant to Texas Agriculture Code, Chapters 161 – 168,
and applicable regulations found in Texas Administrative Code Title 4, Part 2, Chapters 31 – 60 and is eﬀective
immediately.

The Texas Animal Health Commission (TAHC) was established in 1893 as the Livestock Sanitary Commission and charged with protecting the state’s domestic
animals “from all contagious or infectious diseases of a malignant character.” TAHC remains true to this charge while evolving with the times to protect the
health and marketability of all Texas livestock and poultry. Learn more about the TAHC by visiting www.tahc.texas.gov.
