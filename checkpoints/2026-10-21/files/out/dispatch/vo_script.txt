An Austin company's drone, tested by Chevron in the Permian Basin, can fly itself over oil and gas equipment.
Its makers say artificial intelligence in the software sends alerts when it detects anomalies.
On September 30th, 2025, the Environmental Protection Agency approved the system for screening methane leaks.
The drone flies a preset plan to points of interest and records infrared video.
Then a certified inspector reviews the footage to identify any emissions, which triggers a follow-up inspection.
The approval letter mentions artificial intelligence only in the title of a protocol.
EPA has also delayed a separate program for satellite and aircraft leak reports until January 22nd, 2027.
On October 7th it said it will propose to rescind that program.
So in this approval, a person calls the leak. What the software adds isn't in the record.
