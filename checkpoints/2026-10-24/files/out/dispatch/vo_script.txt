In a cardiothoracic suite at Houston Methodist Hospital, four cameras on the walls of each operating room watched each case.
The system timestamped patient entry, draping and room turnover.
It also texted the surgeon when the patient was draped.
A hospital study compared that suite with other Houston Methodist sites and found about 25 more surgeries a month.
That is a rise of 7 percent. The 95 percent confidence interval runs from 8.3 to 41.0 cases a month.
A placebo test did not reach conventional significance.
The authors call the rise associated with the rollout, rather than caused by it.
The rollout came bundled with other workflow changes, and the hospital holds a minority stake in the camera company.
The paper leaves turnover time for future work.
So the study does not show whether the rollout sped up room turnover.
