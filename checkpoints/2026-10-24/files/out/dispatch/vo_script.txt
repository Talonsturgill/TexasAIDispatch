At Houston Methodist Hospital, four cameras on the walls of each operating room watch each case.
The system timestamps patient entry, draping and room turnover.
It also texts the surgeon when the patient is draped.
A hospital study compared that suite with other Houston Methodist sites and found about 25 more surgeries a month.
That is a rise of 7 percent, within a range from 8.3 to 41.0.
A placebo test did not reach conventional significance.
The authors call the rise associated with the system, rather than caused by it.
The rollout came bundled with other workflow changes, and the hospital holds a minority stake in the camera company.
The paper leaves turnover time for future work. So whether the cameras sped up the room is still unanswered.
