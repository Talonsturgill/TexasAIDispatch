UT San Antonio says some people could have ankle-deep water in their yard before a flood warning sounds on their phone.
So its researchers built a flood station that charges itself from light and needs no cell tower.
Four optical sensors at different heights watch the water, along with rain, temperature, humidity and light.
A small chip on the station runs the AI itself, so it judges flood risk without a central server.
When the risk rises, a radio signal can carry more than a half-mile through city streets, and up to five miles in open country, to a hub.
At the hub, a second, larger AI model draws a map of how flooding could spread, for emergency responders.
The university reports ninety eight point eight two percent validation accuracy from early assessments after training, and no field result.
It names no town that has installed one. Deployment is still the goal.
