Texas lawmakers reveal utility company submissions for state-required wildfire plan are lacking
BY  
                                    Michael Lozano
Texas
UPDATED 2:48 PM CT Sep. 25, 2026
PUBLISHED 8:45 AM CT Sep. 25, 2026
PUBLISHED 8:45 AM CDT Sep. 25, 2026
SHARE
TEXAS — As major wildfires become more frequent in Texas, state lawmakers are pushing utility companies to complete plans designed to help prevent and respond to fires near electrical infrastructure.
But with a Jan. 1 deadline approaching, only eight of the 161 utility companies required to submit plans have done so, according to the Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT). 
House Bill 145, passed after devastating wildfires in the Texas Panhandle in 2024, requires utility companies to develop emergency protocols, operating plans for high-risk weather conditions and strategies for managing grass and other vegetation around poles and other electrical equipment.
The Association of Electric Companies of Texas (AECT) helped develop the law. The group says the required plans are extensive and require significant research because of the amount and variety of infrastructure utilities overseen.
“Amount of infrastructure that these companies oversee, whether it’s on the distribution system, within the urban setting, or whether it’s a transmission system in a more rural setting, you know, there’s great exposure. So, that’s why it’s a challenge… and that’s why these plans are so detailed,” said Mark Bell, president and CEO of AECT. 
PUCT, which is responsible for reviewing and approving the plans, told lawmakers that eight companies had submitted plans. Another seven have provided expected filing dates, while 119 have submitted acknowledgments indicating they will complete the required filing.
PUCT will also have to schedule individual meetings with each utility company as part of the review and approval process.
The AECT represents 10 of the utilities working to file plans, including Oncor, the state’s largest utility company. Oncor filed its wildfire mitigation plan on May 26, according to the PUCT website. 
The requirements come as Texas has experienced several significant wildfires, including the Ross Fire in Palo Pinto and Jack counties, which became the second-largest wildfire in North Texas history. 
State lawmakers have said the requirements are intended to take a proactive approach to reducing wildfire risks associated with utility infrastructure.
State Rep. Ken King, R-Canadian, helped spearhead the legislation and serves on the committee that recently discussed the number of utilities that have filed their plans.
During the committee meeting, King said there was no excuse for utilities not to complete the requirement.
Editor's Note: This story has been updated to add that Oncor has filed its wildfire mitigation plan. (Sept. 25, 2026)
