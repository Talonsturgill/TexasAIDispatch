Spring restaurant turns to AI host to stay afloat after server walks out

By Olivia Dance
FOX 26 Houston
Artificial Intelligence
Published October 20, 2026 9:05 AM CDT

Spring restaurant using AI to simplify ordering

AI is transforming the dining experience at Wok & Karahi, a family-run Asian restaurant in Spring! FOX 26 reporter Olivia Dance visited the restaurant to get a first-hand look at the change that took place in just 24 hours after a server walked out.

The Brief

    • After its sole server walked out, Wok & Karahi, a family-run Asian restaurant in Spring, replaced front-of-house staff with an AI platform named Haniya.
    • 100% of customer tips now go directly to the chef and kitchen staff, while ordering is handled via phone, QR codes, and pre-payment.
    • The restaurant reports saving thousands per month now.

SPRING, Texas - Two months ago, the sole server at Wok & Karahi, a family-run Chinese, Indian, and Pakistani restaurant, walked off the job. Rather than shutting down or rushing to post job listings, owner Abbas Zoeb —a former Google software engineer— decided to replace the position with an AI software named Haniya.

"It started out of desperation," said Zoeb. "We could not keep begging one person to show up. Now nobody has to, and our guests and our team are better off for it."

Restaurant's new AI platform

Implementing AI:

Within 24 hours, a virtual host, QR codes and a new pre-pay ordering system took over operations. The digital server speaks seven languages and answers the phone even when the restaurant is closed.

As many independent dining spots across the country struggle with staffing shortages, Wok & Karahi offers a glimpse at the future of fast-casual dining. 

By the numbers:

Today, Zoeb claims the restaurant is saving or winning back $9,650 a month. With about $3,500 of those savings coming from the server salary. 

He says the remaining $6,150 comes from operational efficiencies and reclaimed revenue. 

Kitchen staff and the chef now get 100-percent of customer tips.

The cost to run the AI software is about $300 a month or roughly $10 a day.

Will this work for other businesses?

Casual vs. fine dining limits:

While the setup has stabilized operations at Wok & Karahi, experts note that automation isn’t a one-size-fits-all solution for the hospitality industry. 

Professor Dietrich von Biedenfeld, Supply Chain Manager at the University of Houston Downtown, says: "This seems like a very effective tool for a fast-food restaurant or a quick lunch spot, but for fine dining, the AI is only as good as the prompts."

For casual concepts facing severe labor pressures, Wok & Karahi demonstrates how software can safeguard small business margins. However, the broader question remains whether diners will embrace AI efficiency over human hospitality—or if full automation will remain strictly suited for high-volume, quick-service environments.

The Source: We spoke with Wok & Karahi owner Abbas Zoeb about the new AI software and Professor Dietrich von Biedenfeld.
