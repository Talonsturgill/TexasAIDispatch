 No Servers, No Host, and No Phone Calls: AI Takes Over at a Halal Restaurant in Houston, Texas
 L-R, A table of Wok & Karahi dishes, Chinese and Pakistani side by side. And Haniya, the restaurant's AI host, who answers the phone and serves guests at the table. 
 By Muslim Mirror 
 October 19, 2026 
 SPRING, Texas – Two months ago the only server at Wok & Karahi, a family-run zabihah halal restaurant north of Houston, walked out. The family did not post a job ad. Within 24 hours every table had a QR code, an AI host named Haniya was answering the phone, and every order was being paid for before it reached the kitchen.
 Haniya, the restaurant’s AI server, greets a returning guest by name on the table menu. 
 Nobody has been hired into that job since 
 “It started out of desperation,” said Abbas Zoeb, who runs the restaurant with his family. “We could not keep begging one person to show up. Now nobody has to, and our guests and our team are better off for it.”
 America’s biggest chains are racing to do the same thing. Yum Brands says voice AI now takes orders at more than 890 Taco Bell drive-thrus. McDonald’s has said it will bring AI to all 43,000 of its restaurants. The National Restaurant Association’s 2026 industry report found that 80 percent of US restaurants were short at least one position last year. Those chains have engineering teams and large budgets. Wok & Karahi, which serves Hakka-style Chinese food alongside Indian and Pakistani dishes, has one family.
 A server you can talk to 
 A guest sits down and scans the code on the table. Haniya greets them and asks which language they want: English, Spanish, Urdu, Hindi, Gujarati, Punjabi or Arabic.
 Haniya, the restaurant’s AI server, greets a returning guest by name on the table menu. 
 When a regular opens the menu, she greets them by name: “Welcome back. What can I get you?”
 From there the guest can tap through the menu, or talk to her the way they would talk to a waiter. Say “chicken chow mein” and the dish opens on screen, chicken is selected, and she says “Adding chicken chow mein” as the price updates. Ask what goes well with the karahi and she suggests a pairing, based on what other guests actually order together. She asks only the questions the kitchen needs answered, one at a time, and tells the guest what she added. A note like “no onions” or “nut allergy” prints on the kitchen ticket word for word, because the guest entered it. For guests who find apps hard, older guests especially, there is nothing to learn: hold the button and say what you want.
 Haniya also remembers people. The same host answers the phone, the website and the table, and she knows each guest’s past orders, favourites and rewards across all of them. A regular is treated like a regular. A few hours after a direct order, the guest gets a thank-you text asking how it went. Pickup guests get a text the moment their food is ready.
 Guests pay from their own phone with Apple Pay or Google Pay. There is no waiting for the check, and no dine-and-dash, because nothing is cooked until it is paid for.
 The work nobody sees 
 What the guest sees is the small part. Behind it, the restaurant runs on software the family built with AI tools.
 The phone answers 24 hours a day, including when the restaurant is closed. Late-night catering callers used to find nobody there and take their order elsewhere. Now Haniya takes the request, answers the question or books the order for the next day. She offers an add-on on every call, and about four in ten callers say yes. The AI phone line costs about $5 a day to run.
 Orders from seven places – the counter, the phone, the tables, the website, DoorDash, Uber Eats and Grubhub – land on one screen in the back. Every advertising dollar is traced to the order it brought in. The owner gets a text on every table order and an alert if a ticket fails to print. Every call is recorded and reviewed, and each week the spots where guests got stuck are fixed.
 The money 
 By the family’s own count, the system is worth about $9,650 a month. That includes the server’s $3,500 monthly salary, about $2,000 for each day they used to close when nobody could cover a shift, family members no longer pulled in to work the floor, catering that no longer goes to competitors, and fewer wrong orders.
 The tips changed too. The cooks who make the food now share in them.
 The question everyone asks 
 Does it take jobs? At Wok & Karahi, the job that went away was the one the family could not keep filled. The people who stayed are calmer during the rush, because one person is no longer answering the phone, taking orders, running payments and greeting the door at the same time.
 Zoeb now builds the same kind of system for other independent restaurants through his company, AZ Restaurant Partners ( azrestaurantpartners.com ).
 Wok & Karahi is at 3422 FM 2920 Rd, Unit 120, Spring, Texas 77388. wokandkarahitexas.com
