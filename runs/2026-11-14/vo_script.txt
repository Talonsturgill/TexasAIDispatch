Near Barry, in Navarro County, robots now do the heavy lifting on part of a solar farm. A person still has to set each piece down.
The project is Tormes Solar, 457 megawatts, built by the contractor SOLV Energy for Matrix Renewables. Building it is expected to support about 450 jobs.
On most solar farms, crews set steel tubes in the field first. Then workers lift heavy glass panels onto them, one by one, hundreds of thousands of them.
So Terabase Energy, a company from Berkeley, California, flipped the order. It parks a factory line right in the field.
On that line, robots fasten the panels onto a long steel tube, called a torque tube. The finished piece is a solar table. For now, the line is optimized for one panel and one tracker, made by First Solar and Nextpower.
Then AI-assisted tools check every unit, so a panel in the wrong spot gets caught right away.
Terabase says one line runs on a two minute cycle. Running around the clock, that is more than 20 megawatts a week.
Next, a robot loads the table onto a rover, and the rover drives it out into the field. Tormes is the first site where this version runs with autonomous rovers.
Here is the fair doubt. This is a proof of concept on a portion of the project. Those speed figures are the company's own, and it says real output depends on the site.
The robots also stop short. Workers unload each table and set it on the steel supports waiting in the field.
Terabase says it is building robots for that last step too.
The farm is expected to start running in the first half of 2027. Until then, the robots build the table, and a person sets it down.
