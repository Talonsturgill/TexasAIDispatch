An agreement starts now in Bryan for a laboratory designed to let an experiment choose the next experiment.
The agreement turns that idea into a build plan for an autonomous laboratory.
Robots will melt the metal, shape it, heat-treat it and test what comes back.
The software reads that result. Simulation narrows the choices. The loop decides what sample to make next.
That moves routine fabrication, characterization and computation inside a repeating circuit.
But the hypothesis stays outside. So do interpretation and the metallurgical decisions that drive discovery.
The machines are not running now. The agreement begins the work of building them.
The facility is meant for researchers beyond Texas A&M, in person or remote, through competitive review.
The public record names competitive review. The next thing to watch is who puts the first question into the loop.
