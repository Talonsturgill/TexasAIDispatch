At Rice University, the plan is for AI to propose numerical algorithms. Lean will check their mathematical correctness and cost claims.
The National Science Foundation award starts on the fifteenth.
The project targets numerical linear algebra, machinery beneath simulation, data analysis, and artificial intelligence.
Large language models and reinforcement learning will explore candidate designs efficiently.
But the award says those candidates can be opaque, inconsistent, and different across prompts.
LAAM is planned to represent each candidate as a structured mathematical derivation, not a loose block of code.
The planned gate is Lean, certifying deterministic correctness and computational cost.
If that check succeeds, a machine proposal can become a reusable mathematical object with certificate-backed provenance.
The team plans applications from least squares to matrix completion. This is a funded plan, not a published result.
Open software and public workshops are promised. At Rice, the model gets a pencil. In the plan, proof decides what becomes reusable.
