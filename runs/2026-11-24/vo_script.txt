In Spring, north of Houston, people became the first in the country to order Uber Eats by Zipline drone, on Tuesday, November 17th. Open the app, and pick by drone on the home screen.
Then the drone picks up the order, and flies it to the customer.
As it takes off, its weather sensors measure the wind.
It cruises at about 300 feet, at up to 70 miles an hour. It can carry eight pounds as far as 10 miles.
Rain doesn't ground it. Small microphones listen for nearby aircraft, as far as 1.25 miles away, the company says.
Over the yard, it never lands. It hovers, and the pod comes down on a tether.
Zipline says it stays roughly 330 feet up to deliver. Other delivery drones average 32 feet. Its chief executive, Keller Cliffton, says staying that high keeps it very quiet.
Zipline calls the system fully robotic and autonomous. An Uber executive says it reaches homes in under 10 minutes. Those are the companies' own words.
A person still watches. Zipline's remote pilots can override a flight path if there is a safety issue.
Drone delivery is new to Spring. It is not new to Zipline. In Round Rock, a high school drone team placed and received the first order.
Zipline says it has already made 1 million drone deliveries around Dallas alone.
The goal is 1 million deliveries a day with Uber by 2029. That is a target, not a count.
Back in Spring, a hand takes the sack, and nobody has to tip. The dog still barks, just at the sky now.
