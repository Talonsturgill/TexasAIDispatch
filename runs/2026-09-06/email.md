THE CAPTION, TO POST WITH IT

-----BEGIN CAPTION-----
Before a face can be matched, a camera has to capture a usable image.

At the Progreso International Bridge in Hidalgo County, DHS tested that first step in a live pedestrian walkway. Cameras had to account for walking pace, different heights, hats, sunglasses, umbrellas, daylight and external lighting after dark. The agency says AI helped model the capture zone, observe movement and assess whether facial images were suitable for biometric matching.

Suitable does not mean correct. The agency says pedestrian image capture exceeded expectations, but its public account gives no numerical accuracy rate, no image-retention period and no final selection for future deployment. U.S. citizens were offered an opt-out lane during the assessment.

The test is over. The unanswered questions about accuracy, retention and what moves from assessment into deployment still matter.
