# Before the match

At the Progreso International Bridge, the sourced movement is not a permanent biometric rollout. It is the public account of an operational assessment in a live pedestrian walkway. The film follows one ordinary walk through that distinction.

The camera array has to handle motion, changing height, hats, sunglasses, umbrellas, daylight and external night lighting. The artificial-intelligence layer described by the source models the capture zone, observes pedestrian movement and evaluates whether an image is suitable for biometric matching. Suitability is one stage before a match, not proof that a match is correct.

The turn is what the agency does not publish. Its biometrics office says capture exceeded expectations, but the complete article supplies no numerical accuracy rate, no image-retention period and no final selection for future deployment. The public-facing choice described in the assessment is narrower: U.S. citizens were provided an opt-out lane.

The walk ends. The institutional choice does not. Customs and Border Protection still controls what, if anything, moves from assessment and planning into a future selected system.
