## Leading candidates, ranked

### 1. STAAR automated scoring (wildcard beat) — STRONGEST
Four days old. Every English-language constructed response on STAAR is scored FIRST by an
automated scoring engine; at least 25 percent are then routed to human scorers as a monitor.
On August 14th 2026 those machine-first scores became public A to F letter grades for 1,202
districts and 9,105 campuses.

Arlington ISD (Tarrant County) treats the machine score as a draft. It sent 3,268 writing
responses back for human rescore ahead of the 2026 cycle and 22 percent came back higher.
It has done this as standing practice since TEA began using the engine.

THE PRICE: a rescore request carries a FEE, waived only if the score changes. The right to a
human reader is priced, and the price falls on the district.
THE VOCABULARY: the engine flags a response whose vocabulary does not overlap the training
subset. STAAR Spanish is 100 percent human scored and never touches the engine.
Sources: tea.texas.gov scoring-process PDF (primary, dated December 2023, still the linked
process page), TEA 2026 ratings release (primary, August 14th), aisd.net district news
(primary). Austin ISD fifth consecutive F at two north Austin middle schools, takeover
trigger — journalism (KUT), and NO source says the engine caused those ratings.

### 2. The road — FMCSA warning-triangle waiver
A driverless truck cannot walk back a hundred feet and set three reflective triangles.
FMCSA granted Aurora a THREE MONTH waiver, July 10th to October 9th 2026, for cab-mounted
amber beacons instead, and DENIED the five year exemption. FMCSA's own letter records that
Aurora's study showed drivers responded to beacons less often than to triangles in five of
eight scenarios. Under the previous waiver 34 trucks ran over 500,000 miles and the beacons
were on for a total of about 10 hours.
Aurora: nearly 440,000 driverless miles as of end of June 2026, 10 routes.
COUNTER-IMAGE, in the company's own words: weather constrained Texas driverless operations
roughly 40 percent of the time in 2025.
Atlas/Kodiak, Winkler County: 176 loads of sand in a single day on July 20th 2026, an
all-time high across 18 months of driverless operation.

### 5. Weather, water and emergency — handled without triumph
Guadalupe flooded again July 16th 2026. Comfort (Kendall County) sirens sounded twice, an
hour apart, because people were not leaving; the USGS gauge went 5.46 ft at 5am to 37.05 ft
at 8:05am. In eastern Kerr County no sirens sounded at all. Kerr has six sirens against a
planned 32 to 34; a comprehensive system was estimated at least $14 million against a
$1 million state allotment. Three people died in the July 2026 event.

siren grant program" (TWDB, primary).
