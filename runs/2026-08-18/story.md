# Phase 3 and 3.5 — the story, and why this one

Date 2026-08-18. Beat: **science-machines** (an application beat, and the spine).
Dedupe: FRESH. The ledger is empty, so this is Dispatch number one and nothing is excluded.

## The story

**Who actually gets to use the largest public supercomputer in America, which is in Texas.**

Horizon, the NSF Leadership-Class Computing Facility flagship, is powered on and half a
machine. Its GPU racks are live and its CPU half is not expected until winter or early next
year. A production date was asserted in this repo's own research file and could not be sourced anywhere, so this film never says one. TACC's own user guide, dated the 24th of
July, says the system is limited to internal users. The press release the same month says it
will soon open broadly to the research community. Nobody is lying. The gap between the
announcement and the login prompt is the story.

Two hundred miles northwest at Abilene, the private campus has an estimated fifty thousand
of the same generation of accelerator in a single building, four of eight buildings running,
and no allocation anybody outside the companies can request.

## Why this and not the others

Seven other beats came back and three were nearly as good.

- **STAAR automated scoring** (Tarrant and Travis). Four days old, primary sourced, and the
  strongest pure story of the set: the machine reads a Texas child's paragraph first, and the
  right to a human reader is priced. It is the runner up and it is banked for a later run.
  It lost on one thing only, and it is the right one to lose on. The subject is a scoring
  contract and its best images are a screen and a fee schedule, while this story has two
  buildings, two counties and a physicist, and the difference between them is visible.
- **The FMCSA warning-triangle waiver.** A driverless truck cannot walk back a hundred feet
  and set a triangle, so it runs on a three month permission slip. A lovely irony, and the
  freight kit would have drawn it easily. It is a smaller idea than this one.
- **MD Anderson's Radiation Planning Assistant**, FDA cleared three years ago, built to give
  clinics with no dosimetrist a plan for free, and its own front door still says access is by
  invitation only. That is the same shape as this story told in a smaller room, which is a
  reason to keep it apart rather than to run it.

The deciding argument is that this repo's own `knowledge/texas/APPLICATIONS.md` predicted
this film and got its premise backwards. It says of TACC: *"This is the one data centre in
Texas that is not a private slab behind a berm: it is public, it is academic, and anybody's
research can run on it."* Anybody's research CANNOT run on it, today, and TACC says so in its
own user guide. A show that finds its own doctrine overtaken by the facts and says so is
doing the thing the doctrine was for.

## Phase 3.5 — the earned take

Not "the public supercomputer is late". Late is a schedule.

**The public machine does not have a temple.** It rents a slab in a redeveloped Sears call
centre on a suburban boulevard in Round Rock, in the same county and the same industry as the
private halls the record next door tracks every week. It is not bigger than them. It is not
even close to bigger than them.

**What makes it public is not the hardware. It is the paperwork.** Round Rock publishes what
the campus draws. TACC publishes the queue names, the node caps, the runtime limits and the
rate a researcher is charged. Abilene publishes none of that, and the number everybody quotes
for it comes from an outside analyst's estimate rather than from a meter anyone can read.

So the closing thought is not that the public machine is losing. It is that **the only
machine in Texas whose size you can actually check is the small one**, and the reason you can
check it is that somebody has to be able to ask for time on it.

## The counter-images, and the film shows two of three

1. The user guide that says internal users only, dated, in TACC's own hand.
2. A physicist's group that spent three years rewriting a code for a machine it cannot log
   into, and got its payoff at a two day hackathon on test nodes.
3. The title for most powerful university machine in the country is not held in Austin at
   all. It is held in College Station, by a machine at rank 66. **This one is researched and
   verified and it is NOT in the film.** The script never says it, so the scene carrying it
   was silent, and once the board was cut to the measured read the only room left for it was
   2.4 seconds wedged inside the previous sentence. The measured cost of keeping it was that
   a sentence about Abilene in Taylor County played over a picture of College Station in
   Brazos. One sentence landing on two counties is a worse fault than one counter-image going
   unshown, so the scene is cut and the film carries two. It is banked, with its claims, for a
   run whose script has a line for it.

## The Texas of it

Three counties, three regions, three different lights, and none of them chosen for looks.

| county | region | what is there |
|---|---|---|
| Williamson | blackland | Round Rock, the Sabey campus, the old call centre |
| Taylor | rolling_plains | Abilene, the private campus |
| Travis | blackland | the researcher's bench, the three years of porting |
| ~~Brazos~~ | ~~post_oak~~ | cut with the ranking scene, see the counter-images above |

**A second scene was cut for the same reason.** A generator yard behind the Round Rock block,
silent by design, sat between two sentences the measured read leaves 0.68 seconds between. Two
silent scenes were being wedged into that gap, which is why the word "Horizon" was landing on
the generators two cuts after the building it belongs to. Line one now splits across the two
shots that carry it, the yard and the machine, and the generator beat is banked.

**What the film shows is twelve scenes across three counties.** Not fourteen and not four. The
cuts are here rather than in a note nobody reads, because a planning document that describes a
film nobody rendered is the same fault as a claim that traces to nothing.

`knowledge/texas/` says the newest industry sitting on the oldest landscape is the best
recurring composition this show has. Here the newest industry is sitting on a nineteen
eighties catalogue call centre off a frontage road, which is the same joke one era closer in
and is a thing a Texan drives past without looking.
