In Mesquite, just east of Dallas, a special education teacher opens one student's folder after the last bell.
Inside is the start of an IEP, the plan behind that student's special education services. Teachers there reported it took up to three hours. Per student.
The district runs fifty two campuses with over thirty six thousand students, so those hours stack up fast.
So the district built a tool with Panorama Education. It's called Solara.
It pulls a student's data from district platforms. Academic data, attendance and behavior pour into one draft.
From that data, it drafts the student's present levels of achievement.
It can flag a trend, like attendance slipping, or a subject where the child is struggling.
It was trained on an IEP template, so it lights up the boxes a teacher may have forgotten, instead of making things up.
It won't dictate what help a student needs. It doesn't decide eligibility either. Teachers stay the main drivers of the plan.
Over eleven months, teachers reported the drafting fell from up to three hours to about forty five minutes per student.
Nationally, a 2025 study found AI drafted plans matched teacher written ones in quality, and still saved time after teachers reviewed them.
Not everyone is comfortable. Privacy advocate Amelia Vance warns that the more places information sits, the more places it can be breached from.
She would tell families up front and let them opt out. Mesquite sent no separate notice, saying the tool supports work the district already does.
Panorama says licensed staff still review, revise and finalize every plan. The folder closes before the sun goes down.
