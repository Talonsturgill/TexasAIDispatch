At Houston Methodist, two estimates tried to name the same thing: the patient's discharge date.
At admission, they were close on error. Case managers still named the exact day more often.
At the later checkpoints, the case managers pulled ahead on error.
In the analysis labeled 24 hours, 79.5 percent of their estimates landed within one day. The AI managed 37.9 percent.
That is when the authors say accurate dates are most actionable for bed management.
But they also name a problem with the comparison. Case manager estimates were visible to care teams and may have become partly self-fulfilling.
The AI estimates were accessible in the record, but not enabled automatically.
So the study measured accuracy inside two different workflow conditions. It did not measure downstream decisions or patient outcomes.
The paper calls for prospective, outcome-based testing before judging whether the extra complexity is worth it. That is the next clock to start.
