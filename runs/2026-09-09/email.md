THE CAPTION, TO POST WITH IT

-----BEGIN CAPTION-----
Houston Methodist compared two ways of predicting when a patient would leave the hospital. Near discharge, case managers came within one day 79.5 percent of the time. The artificial intelligence estimate did so 37.9 percent of the time.

That looks like a clear win for people. The study's own caveat makes it more interesting. Case-manager dates were visible to care teams and may have helped shape the outcome, while the artificial intelligence dates were available in the record but not automatically shown.

This was a single-center accuracy study, not a test of better clinical decisions or patient outcomes. The real next step is a prospective test that puts both estimates into comparable workflows and asks what changes for patients.

THE FACTUAL BOUNDARY

The study compared estimation accuracy at one hospital. It did not establish that either method
improved clinical decisions or patient outcomes. Case-manager dates were visible to care teams and
may have been partly self-fulfilling, while the artificial intelligence estimates were available
in the record but not automatically enabled. The film names no unsupported product vendor and ends
on the prospective outcome-based test the authors say is still needed.
