## The pick

**Exxon's robotic drilling rigs in the Permian, at Midland, and the grade the machine had to
beat.** Beat: `oilfield`. County: Midland. Region: `high_plains` per `config/county_regions.json`.

Dedupe: FRESH on both entities and beat. The only Dispatch in the 30 day window is

## Why this one and not the others

Three beats came back and two of them were genuinely strong, so this is a choice rather than a
default.

**Against the road.** Two independent driverless fleets are hauling frac sand in the Permian this
month and the numbers are excellent. It fails on the first law of drawing Texas. The Atlas fleet
works out of the Kermit sand mine, which is Winkler County, but **no source actually said
Winkler**, so the county would be an inference and the region would be chosen for how it looks.
The Aurora lane is properly sourced to Midland and Monahans, but the fleet counts belong to Atlas
and the lane belongs to Aurora, so a film using both would splice two companies into one picture.
The material keeps. It is a better film in three weeks with a county in the source.

**Against farm, ranch and water.** The screwworm drone surveillance is the strongest thing on
that beat and it is nearly a film: named counties, primary USDA and TAHC numbers, a landowner, a
vet. It fails on subject. Its own researcher was straight about it: the thermal targeting is
operating, but USDA still describes the image-analysis AI as exploratory. A show about AI at work
would have to write around the fact that the deciding is being done by a person looking at a
warm shape. That is an honest film about disease surveillance and a dishonest one about AI. The
rest of the beat is April research and a flow-meter grant, and the best available source on pivot
automation says out loud that nobody has commercialised it for row crops. Building a High Plains
irrigation film this week would have been an advertisement.

**For the oilfield.** It is four days old, it is on-scene reporting with a Midland dateline, it
puts a named human being at a screen where a human being used to stand, and every figure sits in
a sentence that can be quoted whole. It is the application layer exactly as
`knowledge/texas/APPLICATIONS.md` defines it: somebody using a tool, at work, in a real place.
And the decision is next door where it belongs, so this show is not the docket's video edition.

## PHASE 3.5 — THE ANGLE ROOM

### What the news says

Exxon has two robotic rigs among more than thirty in the Permian and wants half the fleet
automated by 2028. The pipe handling is done by machine and the man who used to do it is now in an
office on the same rig, working a screen.

That is a summary. It is not a take, and it is the film every trade outlet already made.

### The earned take

**The machine's passing grade was the crew.**

Helmerich and Payne said it on their own earnings call, unprompted, in the plainest possible
words. A robotic rig was brought out with the expectation that it would perform at P50, and
Michael Lennox defined P50 on the call as **at least as well as what our people are performing**.
Out of the gate it exceeded that average. It is now that customer's top rig.

So the exam was written by the men who are leaving the floor. Their average was the line. Nobody
set the bar at what a machine could theoretically do, because there was no such number. The only
benchmark that existed was hands making connections in the weather, and the robot had to clear it
before anyone would pay for it.

### CORRECTION FROM THE VALIDATOR, and it changes how this is said

The first draft of this take said "Exxon's robot beat the humans". **That is not sourced and the
film may not say it.** The Helmerich and Payne transcript never names Exxon as the robotic rig's
customer. Reuters separately names Helmerich and Payne as the contractor that supplied Exxon's
automated rig. Those are two facts and fusing them into one is the film's inference.

So the two halves stay separate and the film is built that way:

- Reuters, at Midland: Exxon has an automated rig, its contractor works robotic machinery from a
  screen, and the floor behind the gate is a red zone. The supplier is Helmerich and Payne.
- Helmerich and Payne, to investors, about a robotic rig: the grade it had to make was its own
  crews' average, and it made it.

The narration says the contractor **had already told investors what a robotic rig has to beat**.
That is true, chronologically right (the call was three weeks before the story), and it does not
claim the two rigs are the same one. The turn survives intact and is now honest.

Three other things the validator killed, recorded so nobody re-adds them: the quarter-of-the-fleet
and half-by-2028 plan, the five robotic rigs by February, and the phrase "super major customer".
None could be pinned to a verbatim sentence. **2028 is not an authorised numeral and cannot be
spoken or drawn.**

### And the second half, which is what keeps it from being a eulogy

**The reason the floor is empty is a drawing of the Grim Reaper on a gate.**

That detail is in the reporting and it is not decoration. Somebody painted a skeleton on the
fence around the drilling floor and wrote RED ZONE on it, because about a third of significant
injuries in the history of well drilling happen in that square of steel. The men who set the
passing grade are also the men that square kept hurting. This is not a story where the technology
is the villain, and a film that plays it that way is lying to a Texan who has worked one.

### The turn the viewer does not see coming

Both of those are about the rig. The turn is what happens when the camera pulls back off it.

**Most Texas operators do not think any of this will make them a dollar.** The Dallas Fed asked
E and P firms how much AI would lower their break-even price per barrel over five years, and the
plurality answer was nothing. Among small firms it was most of them. One services executive put
it in a sentence that belongs in this film: *AI can do much, but AI can not drill a
30,000-foot well.*

So the shape is: the machine cleared the bar the crew set, in the square that was hurting the
crew, and out past the gate the people who own the wells shrug. That is three moves, and the
third one is the one nobody else is making.

### Where the knowledge base pushed it

`knowledge/texas/REGIONS.md` on the High Plains: "This is the region where the newest industry
sits on the oldest landscape most starkly, and that contrast is our best recurring composition."
A robot arm swinging a two thousand pound joint of pipe, with pumpjacks to the horizon behind it,
is that sentence as a frame. The pumpjack is a hundred year old form and it does not look like AI.
That gap is the picture.

`APPLICATIONS.md`: "A number the reader can feel beats a number that is merely large." A third of
serious injuries happening in one square of steel is a number a reader can feel. Two miles of
horizontal hole in a little over six days is not, on its own, and it only earns its place if it
is drawn as distance rather than printed as a figure.

## The honest counter-image

Named, so the scorer can find it: the Dallas Fed survey answer, and the fact that Texas upstream
employment did fall this summer **for reasons that are a price and activity cycle rather than
robots**. The film must not claim the machines did that, and it must not imply it by cutting the
two together without saying so. If the validator cannot stand the employment numbers up, that
line comes out rather than getting softened.

## What can go wrong, written down before it does

- **Midland is the sourced county and the art may not assert another.** Exxon's rigs also work
  Martin, Howard and Reeves. The reporting says Midland. The board says Midland.
- **No brimmed hat on the rig floor.** It is a rubric hard fail and a real safety violation. The
  floor stages `operator` (hard hat over an FR hood) or `engineer` (hard hat). Never `rancher`,
  never `hand`.
- **The P50 line is the film's spine and it lives in one transcript.** If the validator cannot
  find that wording on the page, the take changes rather than the quote getting bent.
