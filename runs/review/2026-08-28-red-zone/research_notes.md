## Beat 1 — THE OILFIELD (the winner, see story.md)

### Lead finding: Exxon's automated rigs, Midland County, August 24th 2026

Reuters on-scene reporting, syndicated in full. A contractor sits in a small office ON the rig
and works robotic machinery from a screen. The pipe he is moving weighs roughly 2,000 pounds.
Outside, a gate around the drilling floor carries a sign reading "Red Zone: Restricted Area"
and a drawing of the Grim Reaper.

Verbatim material available: the red zone sign and the Grim Reaper, the 2,000 pound pipe, "more
than 30 drilling rigs in the Permian Basin, two of which are automated rigs", Bart Cahir's line
that "about a third of significant injuries that occur happen on the rig floor", the two miles
horizontal in a little over six days, and the fleet plan of a quarter next year and half by 2028.

### Corroboration from the other side of the contract: Helmerich and Payne, August 6th 2026

The rig contractor's own fiscal Q3 call. A second FlexRobotics package is now running for a
super major in the Permian. **And the sentence the whole film turns on**: the robotic rig was
brought out with the expectation it would perform at P50, and the call states plainly what P50
means here, "at least as well as what our people are performing". Out of the gate it exceeded
that average, and it is now that customer's top rig.

**The crew wrote the exam the machine had to pass.**

### The honest counter-image, and it is unusually strong

The operators themselves, anonymously, mostly expect nothing. Dallas Fed Energy Survey Q4 2025
asked E&P firms by how much they expect AI to lower their break-even price per barrel over five
years. 62 percent of all E&P respondents expect zero. Among small firms it is 70 percent.

And from the Q2 2026 survey, an oilfield services executive: "Artificial intelligence (AI) can
do much, but AI can not drill a 30,000-foot well."

Upstream headcount is falling, but honestly it is a price and activity cycle rather than robots:
Texas upstream employment fell 1,200 jobs between June and July 2026, and is still up 3,400 on
the year. The film must not claim the robots did that.

### Secondary oilfield findings, not used

- Devon Energy, closed-loop AI autonomously optimising 1,000 producing wells 24 hours a day, up
  from a couple hundred in March. **No county in the source at all**, so it cannot be
  region-locked and cannot lead.
- Diamondback moving to a "pump by exception" company. Good phrase, medium confidence, and the
  transcript's own date is inconsistent with its URL.
- Bridger Gas Mapping LiDAR inventory over 51,785 Permian sites, basin methane loss rate 3.13
  percent, Texas 3.6 against New Mexico 2.1, non-producing facilities the largest contributor at
  38 percent. Peer reviewed and excellent, but **contains no Texas county name**, checked twice.

## Beat 2 — THE ROAD

Genuinely strong and the runner up. Two independent driverless fleets are hauling frac sand in
the Permian in the same month.

- Atlas Energy Solutions and Kodiak, July 31st 2026: 28 driverless trucks across 15 routes as of
  March 31st, approximately 7,000 loads, more than 450,000 tons of sand, over 23,500 driverless
  hours in Q1, an all time daily high of 176 loads on July 20th, and a plan to reach 100 trucks
  by mid 2027. Described by Kodiak's CEO as the world's largest fleet of driverless big rigs.
- Aurora hauling frac sand for Detmar Logistics with nobody in the cab between Midland and
  Monahans, disclosed on the July 29th Q2 call.
- Waymo dropped the Dallas waitlist on August 4th across 50 square miles of central Dallas.
- Senate Transportation heard testimony August 25th: TxDMV has taken 42 complaints on AV
  operations under SB 2807 and has suspended or revoked nobody's authorization.

**Why it did not lead.** The county is the problem. Atlas's mine sits at Kermit, which is Winkler
County, but no source the researcher read actually said Winkler, so the county would be inference
and the first law of drawing Texas forbids that. The Aurora lane is sourced to Midland and
Monahans, which is usable, but the fleet numbers belong to Atlas and the lane belongs to Aurora,
so a film wanting both would be splicing two companies into one picture.

**Held for a future Dispatch.** The material is good and it will keep.
