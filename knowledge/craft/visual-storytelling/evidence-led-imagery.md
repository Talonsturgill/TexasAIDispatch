# Evidence-led imagery

Read the [shared visual-storytelling direction](README.md) first.

## What this approach does

It lets the viewer examine real reporting evidence: a site photograph, source image, dated document, map or published result. It is useful when authentic footage is unavailable and the evidence has concrete visual content.

Choose it when the viewer's question concerns what a document establishes, where a change occurs, who is involved or what a photographed condition actually looks like. It can support an explanatory sequence; it must not turn an entire process story into reading screenshots.

## Research findings and limits

NPR Visuals describes sequential stories combining photography and text, sometimes with audio, video or illustration. Its retrospective reports varied completion performance and cautions that strong visuals and the story itself are essential. Those observations concern interactive web stories and do not establish short-video performance. [NPR's sequential-story analysis](https://blog.apps.npr.org/2015/11/19/sequential-visual-stories.html).

Reuters' standards require accurate images and attribution that identifies outside material's actual source. They prohibit fabrication and warn against visual changes that misrepresent reality. This supports honest identity and presentation; it does not grant licences for Reuters or other publishers' media. [Reuters standards](https://reutersagency.com/about/standards-values/).

## Dispatch method

Give every asset a specific evidentiary job. Before boarding, record the claim it supports, the detail the viewer will inspect, what lies outside its evidentiary scope, and its established reuse rights. A portrait identifies a person. A site image establishes visible conditions at its documented place and time. Neither proves an unseen workflow.

Arrange the evidence around a question:

| Stage | Picture function | Editing choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Orient | Show the actual site, subject or full source context | Hold a readable establishing view |
| Inspect | Reveal the one detail that supports the claim | A purposeful crop or highlight |
| Compare | Show a documented contrast, location or relationship | Matched views with honest identities |
| Establish scope | Identify date, issuer and limitation | A brief readable source passage |
| Return | Connect the evidence to the physical consequence | Return to the subject, changed understanding |

Each cut should answer the previous shot's question. Do not count a new excerpt, title or fade as a new development when it repeats the same information.

For a document, show enough context to establish its identity, then expose the decisive passage. Preserve surrounding qualifications that change the claim. An enlarged crop must not make a planned feature look delivered. Use an original explanatory panel when a truthful transcription is clearer, and disclose it; do not fabricate a screenshot.

For a photograph, keep the actual detail visible. Camera movement should reveal geography, scale or a relationship, not simply animate an inert image. Do not use a crop to imply a different place or eliminate context that changes interpretation. Do not animate a real still into an invented observed event.

## Pair evidence with explanation

If the story says a system processes data, a screenshot of its input supports the input claim only. To explain the transformation, pair it with a disclosed source-bound animation or authenticated output. Do not fill the rest of the film with underlines and excerpts because a source page is easy to obtain.

For the pavement story, a verified example of surface and range imagery could support a comparison. A university article supports attribution and date. It does not supply proof of a completed road repair. When usable technical images have no established rights, draw an explicitly conceptual explanation instead.

Keep complete captions in their measured band. A dense passage and a long spoken sentence can overload the phone frame; use only the necessary excerpt and preserve its source context in the existing evidence package. Narration should explain the visible detail rather than read an unrelated paragraph over it.

## Before voice and final review

The existing critic should explain what each asset proves and where the sequence implies more. Ask whether the film still has a principal action and consequence under the current contract. Reject the known whole-film screenshot-and-fading-box approach before spending.

Keep the prior-edition identity check, native asset provenance, rights evidence and normal phone review. If no useful external imagery is available, omit the insert and move on. The fallback must remain a complete explanatory treatment, not a stalled stock search.
