# Visual storytelling for Dispatch

Research and direction revision: October 2, 2026.

The picture, narration and captions must describe the same unfolding story. The picture supplies a recognizable subject, relationship, action or consequence. Narration explains its significance and limits. Captions give access to the spoken account. A moving object is useful only when the viewer understands what it represents and what its change means.

These dossiers combine researched craft with proposed Dispatch practice. Source observations are identified and linked. The shot methods, selection procedure and worked example are our production recommendations, not claims that research guarantees audience engagement.

## Choose the story's visual approach

Read this index and the chosen dossier in full before boarding. Select by the available evidence and the explanation the viewer needs. Keep one primary approach; supporting footage or documents should connect to that approach through a clear subject or question.

| Approach | Choose when | What it must show | Reject when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Observed documentary](observed-documentary.md) | Authenticated footage shows the actual people, place, equipment or workflow | A real action and its observed result | Similar-looking stock replaces the story's actual identity |
| [Explanatory animation](explanatory-animation.md) | The central process or comparison is hard to see directly | Recognizable input, meaningful transformation and readable output | Abstract objects require a private legend to decode |
| [Evidence-led imagery](evidence-led-imagery.md) | Relevant photographs or documents carry the reporting | What the evidence establishes and how it changes the answer | A sequence of screenshots substitutes for the entire mechanism |
| [Cinematic scene](cinematic-scene.md) | A source-backed physical task, obstacle and consequence can sustain a scene | Spatially coherent action, a turn and a consequential ending | Lighting, orbiting props or music replace story development |

Use this selection order:

1. State the viewer's question, sourced action, affected person or group and honest ending.
2. Inventory inspected, usable assets and demonstrated components. Record what each can and cannot show.
3. Choose the approach that makes the central relationship easiest to understand. Explain the rejected alternative in one sentence.
4. Complete the [sentence-to-shot worksheet](sentence-to-shot.md). Resolve missing pictures before locking narration.
5. Compare exactly two genuinely different complete treatments in the existing shared preview batch. Read the selected dossier for each treatment. Preserve the current contracts, frozen budget and existing independent roles.
6. Have the existing critic inspect the actual rendered sequence before reading the director's explanation. A filled worksheet does not prove comprehension.

Do not impose a weekday rotation. Track the selected approach in the existing creative-direction rationale so the director can see recent repetition. When two approaches fit equally well, variety can break the tie. Never choose variety at the expense of truth or comprehension.

## Give the three channels distinct jobs

**See:** Show the thing being discussed at the moment it is discussed. Preserve its identity across the cut. Make any comparison legible before asking the viewer to infer a result.

**Hear:** Name the relationship, cause or uncertainty that the picture cannot establish alone. Time emphasis and event sound to the actual reveal. A music swell cannot supply an unreported outcome.

**Read:** Retain complete measured captions. Use brief object labels only where they identify a picture or distinction. Avoid simultaneous paragraph copy competing with both the action and captions. Lay out the decisive result above the real caption band and hold it long enough to understand.

Mayer's research summary supports removing distracting content, placing related words near pictures and presenting corresponding animation and narration together. It also reports redundancy effects in multimedia learning. These are learning findings, not a forecast of short-video retention; they do not justify removing accessible captions. Our application is to reduce competing editorial text while maintaining captions. [Cambridge multimedia-learning chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles/C98AB3A6CE760DD63C048936EA0B3B44), [W3C caption guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/captions-prerecorded).

## Review what the viewer receives

Use the existing review assignments and fixed rubric. Add these observations to their existing consolidated notes, not a new scorecard or paid role.

- **Recognition:** Before reading the board, what does the principal object look like? Can the critic name it without guessing?
- **Alignment:** At each narrated clause, does the picture show the named subject and relationship? Record exact film times for contradictions or absent concepts.
- **Development:** What changed, and how does that change lead to the next shot? Distinguish subject transformation from decorative motion.
- **Continuity:** Is the same person, site, sample or document being followed? Is a different example disclosed?
- **Combined comprehension:** After one viewing with audio and captions, what happened, who can act and what remains unproven?
- **Picture contribution:** With narration muted and captions covered, which parts of the central process can the viewer actually see? Dates and attribution may rely on text; the principal action cannot.
- **Ending:** Does the final picture answer the opening question without inventing implementation, savings, approval or repair?

An unrecognizable object, absent principal action or contradictory picture is a comprehension/source defect under the current contract. Do not classify it as merely style because pixels move. Surface polish and optional camera preferences remain artistic issues. Retain all real rejected verdicts and use the existing repair and bounded-release policies.
