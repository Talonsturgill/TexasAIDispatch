# Cinematic scene

Read the [shared visual-storytelling direction](README.md) first.

## What this approach does

It makes a source-backed task and consequence felt through coherent space, purposeful action, composition, light, sound and editing. It can use authenticated footage or an explicitly illustrated reconstruction. Cinematic craft can improve documentary or explanatory work; it is also a primary approach when one scene carries the story.

Choose it when the source supports an actual goal, obstacle, consequential action and honest ending, and the available production method can perform them credibly. Do not invent a named person's feelings or an incident to obtain drama.

## Research findings and limits

Pixar's camera and lighting leads describe reviewing work in story context, shaping a common visual language and choosing camera/lighting contrast to express different character situations in Incredibles 2. Their account makes cinematography a coordinated storytelling decision, not an independent display of rendering quality. It concerns a feature animation; Dispatch cannot borrow its production scale or fictional freedoms. [Pixar cinematography account](https://renderman.pixar.com/stories/incredible-cinematography).

StudioBinder's blocking lesson explains how placement, movement, shapes and space guide attention and emotional interpretation. It is practitioner instruction, not empirical proof that a camera move creates engagement. [Film blocking lesson](https://www.studiobinder.com/filmmaking-techniques-film-blocking/).

## Build a scene around a task

Write the scene in physical terms:

- Someone or something has a source-supported task.
- A visible condition prevents an easy answer.
- An action reveals or changes that condition.
- The revealed consequence changes the viewer's understanding.
- The ending preserves what the reporting leaves unresolved.

The protagonist can be an object followed through a process. Human performance is useful only when it is credible and supported. Avoid adding an inspector with a clipboard solely to suggest stakes.

For a pavement explanation, the obstacle is that a dark surface mark can be ambiguous. The turn is the additional information from depth. The consequence is a better basis for a maintenance decision. The ending must keep the difference between assessment and completed repair visible.

## Direct the camera, light and edit

Establish where the subject is and where the viewer should look. Use a close shot for the decisive detail and a wider shot when the relationship or physical consequence requires it. Maintain orientation across the cut so the viewer does not spend the reveal rebuilding the scene.

Move the camera to reveal previously hidden information, follow a relevant action or shift attention after a decision. Hold it when movement would obscure the result. An orbit around a prop cannot substitute for a turn in the story.

Use lighting to separate the principal subject and make the action readable. A shadow or highlight should reveal a source-supported surface or spatial relationship. Do not use darkness to conceal crude geometry, failed contact or an unreadable subject. Preserve native finish and phone comprehension.

Cut on a meaningful change: contact, revealed information, completed transfer or reaction that is actually supported. Allow the consequence to register. Do not stack unrelated dramatic shots simply because their color palette matches.

Sound should arise from a depicted event, with provenance and disclosure appropriate to the medium. Use music to support pacing and a shift of understanding. Do not let a triumphant sting imply an unreported success. Vocal performance should make the distinction and limit audible without theatrical exaggeration.

## Example and counterexample

A useful illustrated scene might begin close enough to inspect an ambiguous road mark, reveal corresponding depth information in a clearly conceptual view, then widen back to the unchanged road. The action teaches a distinction; the final image retains the physical problem.

A generic futuristic room, floating road tiles, sweeping camera and rising music can look elaborate while communicating no research workflow or human consequence. Adding a cinematic label does not change that result.

A reconstruction must say what it is and stay within established source facts. Do not generate an apparent field recording of named researchers, proprietary equipment or a real deployment when no evidence depicts those events. Original illustration should communicate the explanation while retaining truthful provenance.

## Before voice and final review

Board the opening, central turn and ending as one spatially coherent sequence. Prove the hardest action using demonstrated components before selecting this approach. Budget the complete remaining render and review path; do not build speculative human rigs or a new pipeline to rescue a weak premise.

The existing critic should judge what the character or object does, what changes and why the ending follows. Check actual rendered performance, contact and consequence. An ambiguous mechanism is a comprehension failure, not a surface-style disagreement. Keep optional polish within the current creative cap and retain every real rejected verdict.
