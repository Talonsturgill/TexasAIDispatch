## Production instruction, distinguished from our inference

[NBCU Academy's Joe Fryer lesson](https://nbcuacademy.com/storytelling-broadcast-script/) recommends logging pictures, sound and expressions before writing, finding a focus and recurring thread, and outlining the opening, interview bites and transitions. It describes distributing reveals and using narration for factual information while letting interview moments convey personal response. This is practitioner instruction, not evidence that a fixed rundown improves every short video's retention.

[Brian Champagne's package-writing chapter](https://uen.pressbooks.pub/writingforelectronicmedia/chapter/pkg/) describes precisely matching edited pictures and recorded words, using natural sound to introduce a new thought, and making a reporter's appearance demonstrate or bridge something. Its diamond approach moves from a representative person to the broader issue and back. The anchor introduction and return belong to the surrounding broadcast; a standalone Dispatch does not need to simulate that studio machinery.

## What we take into Dispatch

Our inference from these examples is that a report needs an information sequence the viewer can follow. A presenter, soundbite, document, diagram or location shot earns its place by doing a distinct reporting job. Preserve a recognizable subject while widening the explanation, and bring the ending back to the question that initiated the report.

The examples also show limits: commentary can broaden beyond the initial evidence, an interview can carry most of the explanation in words, and topical footage can supply little explanatory value. We should study those choices critically. Copying a lower third, an anxious introduction or a rapid cut would not solve our own missing picture action.

Use the [reporting structures](news-reporting.md) and [viewer planning method](viewer-plan.md) as our adaptation. The current source, comprehension, picture-action, rights, audio, caption, native-media and shipment contracts remain authoritative. These notes create no new rubric, paid reviewer or production allowance.
