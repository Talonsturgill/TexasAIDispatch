## What the pictures fail to explain

The photo/depth sentence is accompanied by two gray slabs and raised rails. The props are related to pavement, but neither a photo nor a depth map is readily identifiable. The viewer has to learn an unfamiliar model while hearing the central distinction.

The damage/stain frame contains a dark flat shape and a gap in another slab, but the information relationship is represented by suspended profiles. The meaning depends on the director's explanation. A recognizable comparison could teach the distinction more directly.

Repair-report sheets are stylized shapes without readable report structure or priority meaning in the inspected frame. Moving those shapes does not establish what the report tells a maintenance worker.

The date frame retains the same slab-and-report arrangement. Its date is supplied by text; the principal picture offers little additional evidence of who reported the research or when.

The ending retains damaged pavement, which is consistent with the narration's limit. Its weak point is that it remains the same abstract model rather than a clearly felt return to a recognizable physical road.

The overall mistake is treating a source-related prop and an implemented animation as sufficient communication. The needed correction is a clearer explanatory sequence, not additional bevels, particles or camera moves.
