# Observed documentary

Read the [shared visual-storytelling direction](README.md) first.

## What this approach does

It lets the viewer witness the reported work: a technician checking a sample, a machine handling an object, an inspector reaching a decision or a worker responding to a real result. Actual identity and behavior provide specificity that generic illustrative footage cannot supply.

Choose this approach when the actual action is available in usable footage with established reuse rights. A building exterior or spokesperson interview alone does not make an action sequence.

## Research findings and limits

BBC Academy's self-shooting sheet calls for checking sound and picture during capture and getting varied angles and shot sizes. It is practical acquisition guidance, not a complete storytelling method. [BBC self-shooting tips](https://downloads.bbc.co.uk/academy/collegeofproduction/docs/self_shooting_ts.pdf).

Media co-op's five-shot lesson, published by SCVO, uses hands, face, wide view, over-the-shoulder view and another useful detail to answer successive viewer questions. Its exercises are staged training examples; Dispatch's observed footage must not present staged activity as a witnessed news event. [Five-shot sequence](https://scvo.scot/about/work/campaigns/essentialsector/mobile-video-training/support/five-shot-sequence).

BFI's interview with the editors of The Cave describes building around particular characters and situations, then finding both intense events and quieter human scenes. That is a practitioner account of a feature documentary, not evidence that a daily short needs the same structure or scale. [Documentary editing at BFI](https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/praise-undersung-art-documentary-editing).

## Dispatch method

First log usable actions, rather than collecting attractive clips. For each source clip record actor, place, date, starting state, action, visible result, relevant claim and rights evidence. A clip showing someone reading is not evidence that the named AI system made a decision.

Build a compact sequence:

| Shot purpose | Picture | Narration contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Establish the task | Close view of the real object being handled | Name the task and actor |
| Identify the worker | Face or body connected to the same action | Explain responsibility |
| Establish context | Wider shot showing the work site and equipment | Name the site when authenticated |
| Show the operation | Detail or over-the-shoulder view of actual interaction | Explain what changes |
| Show the consequence | Visible output, decision or response from that event | State the result and its limits |

This is coverage logic, not a compulsory five-shot runtime. If the source clip already makes action and result clear, keep it intact. Cut when a new view answers a new question. Preserve screen direction and the object's identity. Do not join unrelated clips into a false before-and-after event.

Use natural sound when its provenance and rights permit it. Distinguish recorded sound from illustrative foley. Keep narration intelligible. Captions follow measured speech; useful speaker identification and meaningful non-speech information belong in accessible captions.

Plan phone framing from the source image. A wide industrial shot may show the correct place yet leave the relevant tool too small. Crop only when the crop preserves context and the action's result. Inspect start, operation and result under the real overlays.

## Example and counterexample

For a pavement-inspection story, useful authenticated footage could show the research vehicle or actual capture equipment, the collected pavement view, and a corresponding analysis result. Each connection needs evidence. Footage of an unrelated road van cannot establish Texas State's workflow. A university headshot can identify a researcher; it cannot demonstrate pavement analysis.

If usable workflow footage is absent, move to explanatory animation or a relevant still. Keep the search bounded. Do not repeat a stock hunt, manufacture a field shoot or label a generated vehicle as the actual equipment.

## Before voice and final review

The existing critic should describe the action from the footage itself: who does what, to which object, and what is visibly different afterward. Record where editing might imply a false location, identity or result. The final audiovisual review should check that narration never claims more than the observed sequence plus cited reporting establish.

Retain capture/asset provenance, rights evidence, trims and explicit archive or illustration disclosures in the existing manifests. Original sound, faces and logos have their own context; an accessible public clip is not automatically a reusable clip.
