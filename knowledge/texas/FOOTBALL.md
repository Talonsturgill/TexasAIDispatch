# Football — the game a Texan measures the week by

The `football.tsx` module draws this. This file is why each object is shaped the way it is, the
history a scene can lean on, and the trademark table the module obeys without exception.

Marks: **[V]** verified against a cited source, **[U]** unverified, verify before a date or a
number built on it ships. The art never needs the unverified facts, because a form is drawable
without a date. A caption or a slide does need them, and an unverified one does not ship.

**The law, before anything else: draw the FORM, never the MARK.** Football in Texas is a thicket
of live trademarks, and the form underneath every one of them is free and carries essentially all
the recognition. A four-bar facemask reads as a lineman with no logo on the clip. A striped
A-frame reads as Whataburger with no word on it. So every surface a real object stamps is left
blank on purpose, and the blank is the law, not laziness. The full table is at the bottom.

**And draw it straight.** Texas football is genuinely absurd in scale, and the absurdity is the
point, but an outsider winking at it reads as contempt and nothing after it lands. The forty
thousand dollar mum and the seventy million dollar high school stadium are drawn with the same
care as a combine. Let the size speak.

---

## Why it carries so much recognition

Friday night is the one thing happening for a hundred miles, and from two miles out the glow of
the stadium lights is visible before the town is. The band outnumbers the team and practised
longer. The drill team, the chain gang, the booster club selling programs, the mums, the
run-through banner: the game is the smallest part of the event, and every one of those pieces is a
drawable object a Texan reads instantly. `hometown.tsx` draws the stadium and the ritual;
`football.tsx` draws the equipment, so a scene can put a helmet on a shelf without staging a field.

**The two Texases are both true and they look nothing alike.** A 2A field with poured-concrete
embankments in the end zones and a two-lane road running past is one building. A 6A district that
spent tens of millions on a video board and a parking structure is another. Drawing only the dusty
one is a nostalgia that flatters itself, and the module carries both (`grandstandBowl` scales from
a modest home stand to a megastadium bowl).

## The history a scene can lean on

- **The UIL** (University Interscholastic League) governs public-school competition and sorts
  schools into conferences 1A through 6A by enrollment, realigned every two years **[V]**. The
  exact enrollment cutoffs and the year 6A was created are **[U]** — the UIL publishes them only
  under paths its robots.txt disallows.
- **Six-man football** is real Texas, not a novelty: small-town and rural schools too small to
  field eleven play a faster, higher-scoring game on a smaller field **[V]**. The year the UIL
  sanctioned it and the current school count are **[U]**.
- **The Prairie View Interscholastic League (PVIL)** governed Black high-school athletics in Texas
  through segregation and produced multiple Pro Football Hall of Fame players including Gene
  Upshaw, Joe Greene, Charley Taylor, Ken Houston and Emmitt Thomas **[V]**. The full alumni roster
  is **[U]** (a source returned it mislabelled). The PVIL is the correct name to invoke for the
  Black-Texas football lineage, and `blacktexas.tsx` is its visual home.
- **The Southwest Conference** is the college lineage most older Texans hold, dissolved in the
  1990s **[V]**. For any SWC diagram use plain city dots, never school insignia.
- **Friday Night Lights** is H.G. Bissinger's 1990 book about Permian High School in Odessa **[V]**.
  Citing the book by author and year is attribution and is fine. The film's title card, its poster,
  and any quoted dialogue are live and are not drawn.
- **Marquee programs** the research surfaced, as culture rather than logos: Permian (Odessa),
  Celina, Aledo, Katy, Southlake Carroll, Duncanville, North Shore, Westlake, Sealy, Refugio,
  Judson **[V as names]**. Title counts and specific years are **[U]** and need a record before
  they ship. The pattern worth drawing is small-town South and West Texas, where a district too
  small to fill one 6A hallway plays at a level the state notices.
- **Stadiums as architecture** (buildings are drawable, naming rights are not): the Astrodome's
  lamella dome, the twin arch trusses and sloped glass of the Arlington megastadium, the Cotton
  Bowl, the Alamodome, Ratliff Stadium in Odessa, Eagle Stadium in Allen **[V as structures]**.

## How to invoke the pride through imagery, without a mark

1. **The light towers at dusk.** Metal-halide glow over an acid-green field, the cones visible in
   humid air. It is the single most-loaded image and it needs no team in it.
2. **The empty stadium in daylight**, seats as a wall. The scale is the feeling.
3. **The equipment at rest**: a helmet on a shelf, pads on a stand, the ball on the fifty. Stillness
   reads as reverence.
4. **The era stamp**: halide vs LED lights, amber vs white scoreboard digits, a painted vs a
   printed-vinyl booster sign. One still frame dates the memory.
5. **The people who are not players**: the drill team's kick line, the band's high-knee, the chain
   gang, the booster at the gate. The event is bigger than the game.
6. **Never** a specific school's colors-plus-monogram, a real mascot, or a quoted slogan. Invent a
   two-color program or use the state flag's lone star, which is a state symbol and not a
   commercial mark.

## The artifacts, and the mistake each corrects

| Artifact | The mistake it corrects |
|---|---|
| `helmet` | a smooth egg with a cage stuck on. Real: a shell with a low crown ridge, a rolled ear with a round earhole, the facemask standing PROUD on two clips, never flat on the front |
| `football` | a rugby ball, too fat and round. Real: a slender prolate spheroid with sharp points, a white lace stripe near each end, pebbled not shiny |
| `shoulderPads` | a vest. Real: an arch over the collarbones with two cantilever caps standing off the shoulders and a laced sternum gap |
| `scoreboard` | a video wall. Real: a steel cabinet on two poles, fixed HOME/GUEST labels with the school names on removable panels, lamp or segment digits. Amber blooming digits read pre-2012, white crisp LED after |
| `chainGang` | one pole, or forgetting it. Real: the down box (a single pole, a flip number) plus the ten-yard chain (two poles, a chain strung taut). The down box is what says football at a distance |
| `blockingSled` | a tackling dummy. Real: a heavy steel skid frame, angled pads on spring arms, runners that gouge the field |
| `waterCooler` | the orange cooler with the bolt (trade dress). Real: an insulated cylinder with a domed lid and a push spigot on a wheeled cart, any neutral color, cone cups on the shelf |
| `drillTeam` | cheerleaders with pom-poms. Real: a precise kick line in the Kilgore Rangerette lineage, hats and boots, the line dead straight |
| `lettermanJacket` | leather-and-felt costume. Real: wool body, leather sleeves, knit collar/cuff/hem in two colors, a chenille letter crusted with pins. Draw the letter blank or a plain block initial |
| `footballAnnual` | lettering the cover. Real: a thick newsprint magazine, a photo cover, a price corner, a year on the spine, hundreds of district-preview pages. The Dave Campbell's form, with NO title (the title is live) |
| `trophy` | a generic loving cup. Real: the metal ten-gallon hat on a plinth, the shape the Red River trophy made famous. Or a plain figure-on-a-column district trophy |
| `endZonePylon` | skipping the small objects. Real: eighteen inches of weighted orange foam. This one IS orange, because the color is a rule and not a mark |
| `grandstandBowl` | only the dusty 2A field, or only the megastadium. Both are true, and the module scales between them |
| `official` | a ref in solid black. Real: a vertical black-and-white stripe shirt, white knickers, a black cap, a whistle and a gold flag. The stripes are the whole read |

## LIVE TRADEMARK TABLE — the mark to avoid, the form to draw instead

Every one of these is a live mark. The form under it is free.

| Live mark | Draw instead |
|---|---|
| Dallas Cowboys star (that blue, that outline, that placement) | a plain five-pointed star in a non-Cowboys color, or the state flag's lone star (a state symbol) |
| The NFL shield | nothing. Omit it |
| Houston Oilers derrick logo and Columbia blue | a four-leg lattice drilling derrick (a real oilfield object); not the stylised logo or the exact blue |
| Any NFL team mark (Texans, etc.) | omit |
| UT longhorn silhouette and burnt orange | a longhorn steer head (an animal is not a mark); not the specific silhouette-plus-burnt-orange pairing |
| Texas A&M, TCU, Baylor, Texas Tech, SMU, Rice, Houston marks | omit all. For an SWC diagram use plain city dots |
| Any high school's mascot mark (Permian's panther, Southlake's dragon, Katy's tiger, Judson's rocket, Aledo's bearcat) | the neutral form only. If a jersey needs lettering, invent a block letter or a number |
| Riddell, Schutt, Xenith, Nike, adidas, Under Armour | the helmet, pad and jersey FORMS are fine; the maker's mark on the clip, the swoosh on the sleeve and the logo on the shell are not. Leave those surfaces blank |
| Gatorade name, the bolt, the orange cooler trade dress | a neutral insulated cooler with a push spigot |
| Dave Campbell's Texas Football title and cover | a thick newsprint annual with a photo cover, a price and a year. Do not letter the title |
| Arlington megastadium name and the telecom globe | the architecture (the arch trusses, the sloped glass, the retractable panel). Buildings are drawable, naming rights are not |
| Astrodome name | the lamella dome geometry. The name is held by the county |
| The Golden Hat trophy | a metal ten-gallon hat on a plinth. Trophy designs can carry rights; the hat form does not |
| Whataburger's striped A-frame | a plain drive-in. The striped A-frame is itself Whataburger trade dress, so it is never generic roadside |
| "Friday Night Lights" as a title, the film's card, its dialogue | the phrase in ordinary descriptive prose is fine; the title treatment, poster and quoted lines are not. Cite the 1990 book by author and year |
| School fight songs and their arrangements | original composition only. Every arrangement is a live work |
| EMS/agency markings on an ambulance | reflective chevrons and a plain white box body, no agency name |

The Texas state flag is a state symbol under its own statutory usage conventions rather than
trademark law. It is drawable and it is genuinely everywhere in this subject. Treat it with the
care you would give any flag, not as a decorative texture.

## What could not be verified (do not build a number or a date on these without a source)

UIL enrollment cutoffs and the year 6A was created. The year the championship moved to the
Arlington megastadium (verified around it: the Alamodome hosted 2002, 2006, 2007, 2009). The year
the UIL sanctioned six-man and the current six-man school count. Whether the six-man field's quoted
80 yards includes end zones. Any specific WPA-built Texas football stadium (the category is
confirmed, the buildings are not). The Dallas Carter forfeiture date and related litigation
(real and heavily reported, but needs a court or newspaper record before a date ships). Refugio's
total title count. Costs of the Katy and McKinney ISD stadiums. The year the Cowboys star was
adopted. Kilgore Rangerette uniform specifics. Absolute dimensions for helmets, pads, chain rods,
bleacher risers, sled mass and turf fiber — every one is described proportionally in the module
rather than with a fabricated number.
