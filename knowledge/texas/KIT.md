# The kit — the everyday objects, drawn maintained but worn

The drawable inventory. **Everything here is "maintained but worn"**: a rust drip, a dent, a
leaning post, a missing letter. Not new, not ruined. New reads as a render. Ruined reads as
apocalypse. Texas is neither.

**Nothing is symmetric.** Vector tooling pulls toward symmetry and real objects resist it. A hat
crease is lopsided. A windmill fan is a full disc but the tail vane swings. A courthouse tower is
centred and the square around it is not.

---

## The industrial kit, which is our actual beat

### Pumpjack

The single most recognisable Texas industrial silhouette. Walking beam, horsehead, counterweight,
pitman arm, samson post.

**The detail that sells a field: every unit is at a different point in its stroke** **[V]**. A row
of synchronised pumpjacks is the tell that nobody looked. Vary phase per unit, deterministically
from position.

Working units are painted and faded. Abandoned ones are frozen mid-stroke and rusted, and an
abandoned unit next to a working one is a whole story with no words.

### Data centre

A **low windowless slab on caliche**. Not a glass tech campus. Long, low, tilt-wall concrete or
metal panel, roof units in rows, a fence, a gate with a card reader, generator yard, and **a
pumpjack or a wind turbine visible behind it**.

That contrast, the newest industry sitting on the oldest landscape, is the recurring composition
this show is built on and it is true rather than editorial.

### Substation and transformer bank

Switchgear, gantry structures, bus work, dead-end structures where the lines land, insulator
strings, and the transformer itself as a ribbed box with radiator fins and bushings on top.

The fins and the bushings are what make it read as a transformer rather than a container. Draw
them.

### Transmission

Lattice steel towers marching to the horizon in the west, monopoles nearer metros. **Conductors
sag in a catenary between towers** and a straight line between tower tops is the tell.

Insulator strings hang vertically at tangent structures and sideways at dead ends.

### Wind turbine

Three blades, one nacelle, tubular tower. **Synchronised red aviation lights at night across the
whole farm** **[V]**, which is an eerie and specific night image.

Blades turn slowly. A fast-spinning turbine is a cartoon.

### Flare, cracking tower, pipe rack, tank farm

The Gulf Coast petrochemical vocabulary. Flare with a visible heat shimmer and a soot tail on a
rich burn. Fractionating columns as tall cylinders with platform rings and stair spirals. Pipe
racks as horizontal ladders of pipe at height, which are the connective tissue of every refinery
frame. Floating-roof tanks with the roof BELOW the rim, which is the detail nobody draws.

### Cooling

Rows of fin-fan or cooling units for a data centre, cooling towers with a drift plume for larger
plants. **The plume is water vapour and it is white and it disperses**, not smoke.

## The rural kit

**Windmill** — the Aermotor-style multi-blade fan on a lattice tower, with a tail vane that swings
to face the wind and a stock tank underneath. The fan is a full disc of many blades, not three.

**Stock tank** — a round steel or earthen tank. In drought it shows a **bathtub ring** and a
cracked bed, and that is the drawable image of a Texas water story.

**Cattle guard** — parallel pipes flush in the road with wing fences either side **[V]**. Drawn
right, it is instantly legible; drawn as a grate, it is wrong.

**Fence** — barbed wire, cedar posts that lean, corner braces with an H or a diagonal. A perfectly
straight fence line is a tell.

**Centre pivot** — a wheeled irrigation arm making a circle, seen from the ground as a receding
line of trusses, from above as the circles that quilt the Panhandle.

**Grain elevator** — concrete silo cluster, the tallest thing for thirty miles, with the co-op
name fading on it.

**Feedlot, sale barn, loading chute, squeeze chute** — the working cattle infrastructure.
**A sale barn is where a hat is real.**

## The civic and everyday kit

**County courthouse on a square** — the most Texas civic image there is. 254 counties, and the
courthouse is the centre of the county seat. Tower centred, square asymmetric, live oaks, a
monument that may be contested (see `CULTURE.md`, monument imagery is a live fight and not
decoration).

**Water tower** — town name and often a state championship year **[U]**. Legs and a bulb, or the
newer single-column type.

**Main street** — one and two storey brick storefronts with a stepped parapet, some restored, some
with a painted ghost sign, some vacant.

**Gas station** — canopy, ice chest, and the specifically Texan version is enormous.

**Taqueria, barbecue joint, dance hall** — a "SOLD OUT" sign flipped in a barbecue window at 1:45pm
**[U]** is a real and beloved detail. Butcher paper instead of a plate, with white bread and raw
onion **[V]**.

**The pink Capitol** — **draw the dome PINK** **[V]**. It is sunset-red granite, not white, and
drawing it white is the single most common Texas mistake in national media. The Great Walk's black
and white diamond pavement is a verified detail **[V]**.

## Vehicles

**Pickup** — the default vehicle. **Clean above the door handles and caked below them** **[U]**,
which is exactly what a real ranch truck looks like and nobody draws. Toolbox across the bed.
Antenna. A dog in the bed only where that is plausible.

**Stock trailer** — slatted aluminium, gooseneck.

**Tanker, workover rig, sand hauler** — the Permian traffic that makes those two-lane roads
dangerous.

**Slab** — Houston car culture. **Chrome elbow wheels protruding a foot past the wheel face**
**[V]**, candy paint, trunk open with the "swangas" showing. Specific, verified, and joyful.

**Combine, tractor, cotton module builder** — the Panhandle and Blackland farm fleet.

**Spend these deliberately.** One or two per Dispatch, placed where the eye already is. Twenty
details scattered through one video is a curio cabinet, not a film.
